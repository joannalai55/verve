# Subtext and humor

## Decode in layers

1. **Words**: paraphrase the literal proposition.
2. **Situation**: identify who is speaking, to whom, after what, and with what stakes.
3. **Signal**: infer attitude, relationship, status move, or emotional defense.
4. **Mechanism**: name the device—irony, understatement, reversal, callback, ambiguity, deadpan, exaggeration, incongruity, or reference.
5. **Effect**: explain why this wording lands differently from a direct statement.

Keep interpretation proportional to evidence. Give competing readings when tone or context could change the meaning.

## Explain beyond the line

Useful questions include:

- What must both people already know?
- What is conspicuously left unsaid?
- Is the sentence's literal meaning compatible with the speaker's behavior?
- Does the speaker gain intimacy, distance, status, or deniability?
- Is the humor in the words, timing, delivery, or audience knowledge?

Translate cultural context without implying that one interpretation represents every native speaker.

## Teach humor as a transferable move

After explaining a joke or sharp line, express its structure abstractly:

- "Treat a dramatic problem like boring administration."
- "Answer the emotional question as though it were a technical one."
- "Agree with the premise, then extend it one step past reason."
- "Use an overly precise detail where a vague answer is expected."

Then create a new, unrelated example and ask the user for one only when they want practice.

## Handle quoted material

Work from user-provided short excerpts. Quote only the minimum needed for analysis. For longer copyrighted lyrics, scripts, or transcripts, summarize surrounding material and analyze short portions rather than reproducing them.
